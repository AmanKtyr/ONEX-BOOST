Clinical Reference Guide

Understanding WHO vs. Asian Indian BMI Cutoffs

Research published by the World Health Organization and the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) indicates that individuals of South Asian origin have a higher percentage of body fat and higher cardiovascular risk at lower BMI scores.

Classification Asian / Indian Cutoff (ICMR) International Cutoff (WHO) Health & Metabolic Risk Recommended Action
Underweight < 18.5 < 18.5 Nutritional deficiency, lower bone density Caloric surplus, progressive resistance training
Normal / Healthy 18.5 – 22.9 18.5 – 24.9 Optimal health & lowest metabolic risk Strength maintenance, cardiovascular conditioning
Overweight 23.0 – 24.9 25.0 – 29.9 Increased risk of diabetes and hypertension Moderate caloric deficit, 10,000 steps, lifting
Obesity (Class I & II) ≥ 25.0 ≥ 30.0 High risk of cardiovascular disease, fatty liver Structured gym regimen, nutritional guidance
Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About BMI & Body Composition

Expert answers compiled for fitness enthusiasts, personal trainers, and gym owners.

How is Body Mass Index (BMI) calculated?

BMI is calculated using the formula: Weight in kilograms divided by Height in meters squared (kg/m²). For example, a person weighing 70 kg with a height of 1.75 meters has a BMI of 70 / (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86 kg/m². For imperial measurements, the formula is: [Weight (lbs) × 703] / [Height (inches)²].

Why are Indian and Asian BMI thresholds lower than WHO standards?

South Asian individuals naturally have a higher percentage of body fat and visceral fat around internal organs compared to other ethnicities with the same BMI. As a result, the risk for Type-2 Diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome increases at a lower threshold (BMI 23.0 instead of 25.0).

What is the difference between BMI, BMR, and TDEE?

BMI (Body Mass Index) evaluates weight relative to height.
BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the baseline calories your body expends purely at rest to sustain brain, heart, and lung function.
TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your total daily calorie burn when accounting for your daily physical activity and gym workouts. Eating below TDEE leads to fat loss; eating above TDEE with resistance training promotes muscle hypertrophy.

Why can bodybuilders and muscular athletes register as "Overweight" on BMI?

BMI only looks at total body scale weight and cannot distinguish between dense lean muscle tissue and fat. A 5'10" competitive bodybuilder weighing 90 kg with 10% body fat will register a BMI of 28.5 (technically "Overweight"), even though they are extremely lean and physically fit. In strength training communities, skinfold calipers, DEXA scans, and waist-to-height ratio are superior body composition indicators.
